When a negative review belongs on the profile

A customer writes that their appointment started late and they would choose a different provider next time. The business may remember the schedule differently. A calm reply and a review of the appointment process are usually the more useful options unless separate facts support a policy issue. If the same post also exposes someone’s private medical information, assess that disclosure on its own.

What to save before reporting

  1. Copy the review URL and the matching Google Business Profile. Record the location, date and exact words that relate to the concern.
  2. Write down the policy category and one or two verifiable facts that connect it to the review. Distinguish what you observed from what you suspect.
  3. Save supporting material privately. Share only the relevant portions if the available reporting route allows documents. Keep passwords and unrelated employee or customer information out of the report.

The review checker can help identify a policy concern, and its evidence worksheet helps organise what you already know.

Choose a reporting route for the concern

For an ordinary review policy issue, start with the Business Profile or Google’s Reviews Management Tool. If a report has already been declined, check whether a one-time appeal is available and explain the particular evidence you want reviewed. If there is a demand for money or favours linked to the posts, gather the messages and use the dedicated extortion route. Our reporting and appeal guide explains the steps and status labels.
