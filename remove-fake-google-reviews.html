What makes a Google review look fake?

Google’s policy addresses fabricated experiences and manipulated engagement. A one-star rating, an unfamiliar display name or a customer who cannot be found in one database is not enough to establish that a review is fabricated. People can use a different name, buy as part of a group or describe an interaction that did not produce a sale. Google Maps content policy.

Start by separating what the post says from what you can independently check. “We do not recognize this name” is an observation. “This person is a competitor” needs evidence of that relationship. Your report should make the distinction visible.

Handling several suspicious Google reviews

For a sudden cluster, create a chronology with the listing, each review URL, date observed, wording and any related message. Record separate reports and outcomes rather than treating a whole cluster as one case. State which facts suggest a link between posts and which remain uncertain.

If you manage several Canadian locations, keep the profile URL and city beside every review. This makes a Toronto branch, a Calgary location and a Vancouver listing distinguishable without assuming the same reporting outcome for all of them. A screenshot should preserve the connection to the relevant review, not simply show an isolated star rating.

Report the specific issue and track the decision

Use the ordinary reporting route for a documented policy concern. After submitting, keep the reference and check the status before deciding on the next step. Our Google report and appeal guide explains how to distinguish a pending assessment from a reviewed decision. Official reporting guidance.

Prepare a short factual summary: the review URL, the policy concern, the exact passage or behaviour, and the evidence that supports it. An unsupported description of the reviewer’s motives adds less than a clear explanation of the facts.

What if someone offers to delete reviews for money?

Preserve the communication and relevant review URLs. Google provides a dedicated route for direct demands for payment or favours tied to negative-review removal. The sender, timestamps and sequence of events matter. Do not pay the person making the demand; Google’s extortion guidance explains what to submit.

A burst of low ratings without a demand does not by itself establish extortion. Keep that distinction when documenting the case.

Will removing a one-star review change the rating?

That depends on the number of ratings and the underlying average. A displayed rating is rounded, so a single removal does not always change the visible number. Our 1,625-scenario rating study explains the model and uncertainty. It models arithmetic, not removal eligibility.
